The ceiling nobody can raise by trying harder

Only 824 of 1,797 offers (45.9%) carry a single published specification. That is the hard limit on every spec-derived column in the brief. Broken down by source: ExportersIndia 198/198 (100%), IndiaMART 626/1,415 (44%), and BigBMart 0/184 — every one of its listings ships an empty spec object.

A second ceiling applies to steel specifically: all 64 TMT listings arrive with no specs at all, so every TMT attribute — diameter, mass per metre, rod weight, mill type — is parsed out of the title or computed from IS 1786 engineering constants. There is no steel spec data to mine. That is a gap in what the source publishes, not in the parser.

One live column that must not be oversold

Certification looks 100% filled and is not a certification claim. The breakdown is BRAND_LICENSED 765, NOT_APPLICABLE 559, NOT_DECLARED 472 — and CERTIFIED: 1. Exactly one offer in the whole catalogue publishes a licence number. BRAND_LICENSED means "this manufacturer holds a BIS licence for this IS code, but this listing does not quote it", which is a weaker claim and is rendered as BIS* rather than as a tick. A "BIS certified only" filter marketed on this column would misrepresent 764 listings.

Why the seller's brand is not promoted into the brand column

The audit suggested merging Brand Name into product.brand to lift it off 46%. That would have been wrong. brand feeds item_code, which is product identity — one mis-declared brand there splits a product in two or merges two into one, and sellers do contradict themselves. One collected listing reads title "Maha PPC Cement", Brand "Birla Cement", Type "Maha PPC Cement". So the seller's typed brand is kept as a separate quoted attribute, shown beside the resolved brand, and identity stays roster-gated.

Needs time — the schema is ready, the data is not

price_history holds 20,611 rows, which looks healthy until you count the distinct timestamps: 20, all inside a single 14-hour window. That is one observation sampled twenty times, not twenty days of market data. Every trend column in the brief depends on it.

Three claims in the brief that the data contradicts

Warranty does not exist. The word appears 0 times across all 4,625 collected listings. Warranty Duration, Return Policy and Replacement Available have nothing behind them. Shelf Life is present on 19 products and is a different thing.

Availability is not a two-state signal. stock_state is unknown on 100% of loaded offers. The archive does carry on_request — but only on listings with no published price, which the loader deliberately skips, because a price with no basis cannot be shown. So In Stock / Out of Stock / Pre-order cannot be filtered, and Stock Quantity is published by nobody at all.

Discount % works for one tenth of the catalogue. An MRP is needed to compute one, and mrp_paise is filled on 182 of 1,797 offers — 98.9% of BigBMart's listings and 0% of every other source. It ships as an optional MRP column rather than a headline discount badge, because a "20% off" badge that can only ever appear on one platform's stock is a ranking distortion, not a feature.

How the table itself stays honest

Sorting is server-side. A header click changes the query and refetches, so the order applies to every matching result rather than to the 24 rows the browser happens to hold. Sorting client side would have made page 2 disagree with page 1, and the header disagree with the sort dropdown.

The disclosed order is the applied order. When a column drives the sort, the line under the controls names that column and its direction — "Unit price · lowest first", "Seller · A–Z" — instead of continuing to say "Recommended". CP (E-Commerce) Rule 5(3)(f) requires the ranking parameters shown to a customer to be the ones in force.

Nulls sort last in both directions. "Unknown" is not a value at either end of a range: sorting rating descending should surface the best-rated sellers, not the 49% who publish no rating.

Every column carries its coverage. Patchy columns print their percentage in the header and in the column picker, measured over the results on screen.
